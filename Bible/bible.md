# Story Bible — *Seen and Still Loved*

Facts extracted from the manuscript. Organised by category. Updated in segments as chapters are read.

**Coverage:** Chapters 1–9 (Part 1: Hiding)

---

## Characters

### Ethan Calloway
- **Role:** Protagonist / love interest
- **Profession:** Exhibition designer at the City Museum of Contemporary Art
- **Photography:** Amateur photographer, shoots black-and-white film. Has his own darkroom (a converted bathroom his mother built). Takes street portraits and candid images, primarily of people and the space *between* people.
- **Self-portraits:** Has been making self-portraits for two years. He finds them all technically correct but "essentially false" — they look like someone performing the idea of himself rather than being himself. By Ch. 7 the most recent one begins to look like someone "in the middle of understanding something."
- **Personality:** Patient, observant, says what he means, not prone to flattery. Tends to wait rather than fill silences. Files information rather than discarding it.
- **Mother:** A passionate amateur photographer who died when Ethan was nineteen from a sudden aneurysm. She built the darkroom. She believed that photographs were "the only honest record of a moment." She photographed the *space between people* and believed that space was where all the real things happened. She had a personal, non-churchgoing faith that she described as a belief in something that held things together. Her portrait (smiling, in a silver frame) sits on a shelf above Ethan's desk.
- **Living situation:** Owns a house. Books sorted by subject. A large black-and-white print on the living room wall — a woman with her back to the camera, standing at a window, light across her shoulders. Prints clipped to a wire running the length of the hallway (the wire made from coat hangers and eye hooks). A kitchen that has been cooked in.
- **Faith:** Does not know what he believes. Thinks about it. His mother's faith is a reference point for him.
- **Key action (Ch. 5):** When his hand finds that Maya is trans, he goes still — not recoiling — and after three seconds asks "Is it okay if I hold it?" He spends the entirety of Ch. 5 choosing her, without drama.
- **Key failure (Ch. 7):** After telling Jake what happened, he lets Jake's question about his sexuality introduce doubt. He spends a full day in silence — not calling Maya — before driving to her apartment. He apologises on the kitchen floor. He promises: "Whatever is happening — I come to you and we face it. No more silence."
- **Photography note:** He photographed Maya in Gallery Six the night they met, before he spoke to her. He tells her later: "You looked so alive. I'd been working in that museum for two years and I'd never seen anyone look at a painting like that."

### Maya Vasquez
- **Role:** Protagonist
- **Identity:** A trans woman. This is established gradually and physically in Ch. 5.
- **Profession:** Part-time gallery guide at the City Museum of Contemporary Art; PhD candidate
- **Thesis:** "Hidden Narratives in Abstract Expressionism" — arguing that certain artists embedded personal histories into their *technique* rather than their subject matter. Key case study: Elara Voss. Framework: the technique itself is the narrative; concealment is a form of honesty, a slower kind.
- **Personality:** Private, precise, manages herself carefully in public. Has a "managing voice" she has carried since she was seventeen — it has ended every relationship before it could find her out. She challenges people when she suspects they're not being genuine ("Do you actually, or are you just saying that?"). When she decides something she doesn't hedge.
- **The real smile:** Has two modes — measured public politeness, and an unrehearsed real smile that changes her face entirely. Ethan notices it immediately on the first night and catalogues it.
- **Roommate:** Zoe (see below). They always end up on the kitchen floor with bad wine when something real has happened.
- **Past relationship:** Someone named Bret. He looked at her "with disgust" — strongly implied this was a trans-disclosure moment that went badly. This is the template against which she measures every relationship and every moment of vulnerability. Zoe tells her: "He's not Bret."
- **Faith:** Used to believe. Stopped after whatever happened (Bret-adjacent, or the same event). It is "complicated." Her grandmother modelled quiet, uncomplicated faith. By the end of Ch. 6, she lies in bed after being known fully by Ethan and begins, for the first time in years, to not immediately dismantle the idea that God might also be able to do what Ethan just did.
- **Grandmother:** A retired schoolteacher from County Kilkenny. Moved to the city when Maya's mother was small and never quite adjusted to it. Kept a south-facing balcony garden and grew things that had no business growing in a city apartment. Died two years before Ch. 4, quietly in her sleep. Had a simple, unchallenged faith — described God not as a judge but as "someone who already knew you completely and had decided you were worth knowing." Said to Maya when she was twelve, after she scraped her knee on the church steps: *"He sees all of you, love. Not the parts you show. All of it."*
- **Mother:** Mentioned only in contrast — Maya has been twenty-six years with no connection to faith or church, and her mother is implied to be part of that distance. No direct description yet.
- **Academic work:** Thesis presentation given in Ch. 8, attended by Dr. Bowen and faculty. Maya is at home at a lectern — "completely without fear" when she's not afraid. During the Voss analysis she realises she has been doing to God what the artists she studies did with their pain: hiding, encoding, deciding the answer was no before asking.
- **Photography:** Beginning to develop an eye. She shoots a forty-minute wait at her apartment window to catch the right quality of morning light and sends it to Ethan.

### Zoe
- **Role:** Maya's roommate and best friend
- **Personality:** Warm, direct, steady. Gives Maya room but is not afraid to push. Her way of pushing is quiet — she says the hard thing once and lets it sit. She knows when to leave (she picks up her keys and goes during the kitchen-floor confrontation in Ch. 7, giving them the whole night).
- **Her fierce certainty (Ch. 6):** "You deserved that. Not someday. Not eventually. Not when you'd earned it or when the situation was right or when you'd found some way to make it easier for whoever was on the receiving end."
- **Rachel connection:** She is the link between Rachel and Maya — Rachel asks Zoe for the thesis presentation time when Ethan asks Rachel.

---

### Jake
- **Role:** Ethan's closest friend, three years of friendship
- **Physical:** Tall and loud in the way that makes rooms feel smaller.
- **Personality:** Thinks of himself as a caring friend; is warm and attentive in company. Not malicious.
- **Key action (Ch. 7):** After Ethan tells him what happened, Jake asks "Did you enjoy it?" and then raises the possibility that Ethan is bisexual and needs to examine himself before returning to Maya. He intends this as protective. It causes the silence. He acknowledges this to Maya in Ch. 9: "I was putting my discomfort into language that sounded like care."
- **Key growth:** He sends Maya a card. He arranges a one-on-one coffee with her. In that meeting he receives her full account of what his words did and sits with it without defending himself. He arrives somewhere he hadn't been before — not performing the work, actually doing it.
- **Pub (Ch. 8):** Points at Maya and gives Ethan a thumbs up from across the table, which is subtle to no one. Says to Ethan: "She's the same at this table as she was when you described her at the museum. A lot of people aren't."
- **Partner:** Priya (see below).

### Priya
- **Role:** Jake's partner
- **Profession:** Environmental lawyer
- **Personality:** Strong opinions on most things. No patience for anyone who doesn't bring their full self to a conversation. She and Maya click immediately, deep in urban water rights within four minutes.
- **Introduction:** Pub scene, Ch. 8.

---

### Rachel Calloway
- **Role:** Ethan's **sister** / supporting character
- **First appears on-page:** Chapter 11
- **Physical:** Shorter than Ethan by five inches; same dark eyes; wears a paint-stained flannel shirt over a dress — not an oversight
- **Profession:** Middle school art teacher
- **Personality:** Forward, warm, direct. Fills a room before she's taken off her coat. Asks real questions, not polite ones. Says things plainly and lets them land.
- **Faith:** Still attends church occasionally — a small progressive congregation on the north side. Their mother took them to church as children. Ethan has drifted but never entirely closed the door.
- **Key action (Ch. 11):** Arrives for the weekend; hosts brunch with Ethan and Maya; later texts Maya separately for a private coffee meeting. Tells Maya: *"I'm glad it's you. Properly glad."* Confirms Ethan was the one who found their mother.
- **Key action (Ch. 8):** Sends Jake the pointed text about his silence after Ch. 7, naming exactly what he cost both Ethan and Maya.

---

### Dr. Bowen
- **Role:** Maya's academic supervisor / thesis advisor
- **Personality:** Has a specific expression when genuinely interested rather than professionally attentive (Maya can read the difference). Sends Maya a "nod that meant: yes. exactly that" at the end of the thesis presentation.
- **Brief appearance:** Ch. 8, second row of the conference room.

---

### Dennis
- **Role:** Night security guard at the museum
- **Description:** A large man. Eleven years on the night shift. Has seen stranger things. Delivers the closing-time message to Ethan and Maya with appropriate gravity: "Building closes at eleven. Just so you know."
- **Appears:** Ch. 1.

---

### Elara Voss
- **Role:** Historical artist (fictional); central case study in Maya's thesis
- **Facts established:** German, 1920s, almost completely unknown. Every painting has an area in the lower left corner — the most worked area on the canvas, layers built up over weeks. Voss called it *the true thing* in her diaries. The rest of the painting was for the viewer; this part was hers. She encoded dates, initials, and once (1924) a tiny closed door with no handle. Maya argues the *true thing* is the actual subject; everything else is misdirection.

## Locations

### City Museum of Contemporary Art
- The primary workplace for both Ethan (exhibition design) and Maya (part-time gallery guide).
- **Gallery Six:** Where Ethan and Maya first meet. Contains a mid-sized oil painting by Diaz (newer acquisition, director quietly proud of it) and a smaller Winters charcoal on the adjacent wall.
- **Gallery Seven:** Where Ethan is fixing the lighting on the night they meet. The lighting is "too warm, flattening the texture of the larger canvases instead of pulling it forward."
- **Reading room:** Off the east corridor. A low bench facing a wall of archival black-and-white photographs — "grainy images of men in suits standing in empty rooms, pointing at walls." Maya calls it honest: "It's not trying to be a gallery. It's just a room with pictures of people who cared about something."
- **East courtyard:** Warm for October; they have coffee there in Ch. 3.
- **The Hartley retrospective:** A major six-week installation Ethan is working on when the story opens. It is two days behind schedule, opening in nine days.

### The Palette
- A coffee shop: local art on every wall, Edison bulbs on dimmers, dark roast, something baking. The owner knows Maya and will leave them alone. Used for their first daytime coffee date (Ch. 3, a Saturday, 2pm).

### The hiking trail on the ridge
- Harder than it looks. Beautiful in a way that makes you angry. They start at 7:30 AM; Maya arrives first with two coffees. An exposed ridge section. An overlook where the city is visible below, the river a grey ribbon, the museum visible in the downtown cluster. A hawk turns circles. This is where they first kiss (Ch. 4).

### Ethan's house
- Where he lives alone. The darkroom is a converted bathroom, built by his mother. Contains: a print on the living room wall (woman at window, back to camera, light on shoulders), prints clipped to a wire the length of the hallway, a kitchen that has been cooked in. First visited by Maya in Ch. 5.

### Maya and Zoe's apartment
- Not in detail. A kitchen where they stand and talk. A couch. Her room. The kitchen floor is where serious conversations happen (with bad wine). Scene of the kitchen-floor reconciliation (Ch. 7). Zoe can hear Maya come in late.

### Riverside Community Church, Clement Street
- Maya passes it walking home in Ch. 1. The lights are still on, a meeting in progress, people setting out chairs. She used to belong to a place that felt like that. She hasn't been inside a church "like that" since she was sixteen. She doesn't stop, but she looks.

### The conference room (university)
- Holds thirty people. Scene of Maya's thesis presentation (Ch. 8). Dr. Bowen in the second row.

### The pub
- Jake's idea. Loud. Good jukebox. Excellent beer. First time Maya meets Jake and Priya together (Ch. 8).

### The café near Maya's campus
- Jake's chosen location for his one-on-one with Maya (Ch. 9). Out of his way, table away from the window. Deliberate choices.

## Relationships

- **Ethan ↔ Maya:** Meet in Ch. 1. First kiss Ch. 4. First intimate encounter Ch. 5. Survive their first real crisis (the Silence) in Ch. 7. By the end of Part 1 they are established, the crisis resolved, and Jake has made his amends.
- **Maya ↔ Zoe:** Long-standing, deeply trusting friendship. Zoe is the only person Maya tells everything. Has known about Bret; has been watching Maya's relationship patterns for two years.
- **Ethan ↔ Jake:** Eight years. Jake is loyal and tends toward bluntness disguised as care. He is Ethan's person the way Zoe is Maya's.
- **Jake ↔ Priya:** Partners. Priya has strong opinions; Jake defers to them in most things.
- **Rachel ↔ the group:** Connected to both Ethan and Zoe; protective and clear-eyed. Has not appeared on-page yet.

## Key Facts and Established Truths

1. Maya is a trans woman. She has never been disclosed to a partner without it ending badly (Bret). Ethan is the first person who responds with acceptance.
2. Maya's faith collapsed at some point in her teens/early adulthood — likely connected to Bret. She stopped believing and has been "exiled" from it since.
3. Ethan's mother is a foundational figure for him. Her death at his age of 19 from an aneurysm shaped his photography and his understanding of connection. Her belief system (connections are real in a way that matters; the space between people is where real things happen) is a kind of inherited theology for him.
4. Maya's grandmother carried a quiet, undefended faith. Her phrase: *"He sees all of you, love. Not the parts you show. All of it."*
5. Elara Voss and the concept of the *true thing* — art that encodes personal truth in technique rather than subject — is the lens through which Maya understands both art and, increasingly, herself and her relationship to God.
6. Ethan takes all his photographs in black and white, on film.
7. The self-portraits: Ethan has been making them for two years. They have all been "essentially false." This is an ongoing motif.
8. Bret: named but not yet described in detail. The consequence of him is fully present.
9. Maya's managing voice: she has been running it since she was seventeen. In Ch. 5 she tells it to be quiet for the first time. This is described as something she has never done before.
10. Ethan's promise to Maya (Ch. 7): "Whatever is happening — I come to you and we face it. No more silence."
11. Maya walked past Riverside Community Church at the end of Ch. 1. She hasn't been inside a church that felt like that since she was sixteen.
12. Jake's failure is not malice — it is discomfort dressed as care. He knows this by the end of Ch. 9.

## Themes and Motifs

- **Hiddenness and revelation:** Maya's thesis on concealment in art mirrors her own life. The question of whether hidden things can be revealed without destruction.
- **Light and seeing:** Ethan works with light professionally. The metaphor recurs — the right light doesn't just show what's there, it shows "what was always there but hidden." (His mother's phrase.)
- **The space between people:** His mother's subject. Where all the real things happen. The distance between disclosure and acceptance.
- **The *true thing*:** Elara Voss's name for the hidden authentic thing in the lower left corner. Used implicitly throughout to describe what Maya has never shown anyone.
- **Being known and still wanted:** The story's central emotional question, as stated by the title. The fear that complete knowledge equals rejection.
- **Photography as a seeing practice:** Both characters are learning to see. Ethan teaches Maya about changing your angle. She teaches him about the hidden parts being the real subject.
- **Faith as a thread:** Introduced in the midnight phone call (Ch. 3), developed through Maya's grandmother and Riverside Community Church, becomes a tentative internal question by end of Ch. 6. The parallelism: if Ethan can see all of her and not flinch, maybe God can too.
- **The self-portrait:** Ethan's two-year inability to photograph himself honestly. A running metaphor for his own concealment.

## Locations

### City Museum of Contemporary Art
- Gallery Six (Diaz painting, Winters charcoal); Gallery Seven (Hartley retrospective lighting); Gallery Four (Hargreaves installation); reading room off east corridor; east courtyard

### The Palette
- Maya and Ethan's first coffee date. Owner knows Maya.

### The hiking trail on the ridge
- Start time 7:30 AM, October. The overlook: flat rock, long view of the city, the river as a grey ribbon, the hawk. Site of the first kiss (Ch. 4) and the proposal (Ch. 27).

### Ethan's house
- Darkroom (converted bathroom, mother's build); print wire in hallway; large print on living room wall (woman at window). By Part 3 Maya has started thinking of it as her kitchen.

### Maya and Zoe's apartment
- Kitchen floor is where serious conversations happen (with bad wine). Coat hooks. Her desk.

### Riverside Community Church, Clement Street
- Maya first passes it Ch. 1. Goes inside first time Ch. 21 (since grandmother's funeral at 17). Clear glass windows, plain wooden pews, stone walls, old wood smell. Sixty to seventy in the congregation. Wednesday evening service, 7pm. Site of the wedding.

### The Hidden Gallery
- Directed by Celia. Grey blazer to every event. Site of the *Concealed/Revealed* show (February, 37 pieces) and the portrait show (April, 42 photographs).

### St. Anne's basement
- Nancy's Wednesday evening support group. 7pm. Door on the side street. Six chairs in a rough circle, two floor lamps from home, thermos of tea, biscuits.

### The university conference room
- Maya's thesis presentation (Ch. 8). Holds thirty people.

### Café near Maya's campus
- Jake's choice for the one-on-one apology (Ch. 9). Out of his way; table away from the window.

### The café on Millbank corner
- Eileen's regular café. Green tiles. Scene of the engagement announcement to Eileen (Ch. 28).

## Established Facts

1. Maya is intersex — specifically CAIS (Complete Androgen Insensitivity Syndrome). She cannot get pregnant.
2. Maya's mother Eileen was told about her condition when Maya was three weeks old and said nothing for twenty-six years.
3. Maya found the medical term herself at sixteen in a school library.
4. Bret was an arts critic/journalist. After Maya disclosed to him he said: *"You're a freak. You're not a real woman. You deceived me."* He threatened to use his professional platform against her.
5. Maya's faith collapsed the same night as the Bret disclosure. She had prayed the night before; the prayer appeared to go unanswered.
6. Maya has carried a Post-it with Psalm 139:14 in her own handwriting since she was sixteen.
7. Ethan was the one who found his mother. He was home for the weekend.
8. The ring: same shade of green as his mother's moss-agate brooch. Not planned — he discovered this after the purchase.
9. The best photograph Ethan has ever taken is of Maya at the portrait exhibition, looking at herself at the east wall, her posture entirely open.
10. The wedding is at Riverside Community Church; Ethan's photographs of Maya are hung on the walls of the nave.
11. Maya's father walked her down the aisle, having called and asked three weeks before the wedding.
12. Rachel is the best person at the wedding.
13. Elara Voss called the worked corner of each canvas *the true thing*.
14. Psalm 139 appears three times: the Post-it note (Maya's own handwriting), the gallery piece titled *Psalm 139* (collage), and Pastor James's sermon.
15. The title is earned through the novel's central metaphor: Maya, fully in the frame — seen completely, hidden from nothing, and still chosen.
16. The managing voice: built since Maya was seventeen; told to be quiet for the first time in Ch. 5; largely gone by the end of the story.

## Themes and Motifs

- **Hiddenness and revelation:** Maya's thesis mirrors her life. The question of whether hidden things can be revealed without destruction — answered, definitively, yes.
- **Light and seeing:** Ethan works with light professionally. The right light shows "what was always there but hidden." The photographic act as an act of love.
- **The space between people:** His mother's subject. Where all the real things happen. By the end, Maya no longer needs the space — she has closed the distance.
- **The *true thing*:** Elara Voss's name for the hidden authentic thing. Applied to Maya, to Ethan's self-portraits, to the thesis, to the prayer.
- **Being known and still wanted:** The title's promise. Paid off in the final image: her hand in his, stepping into the ordinary world, the door swinging shut.
- **Photography as a seeing practice:** Both characters learn to see. The portrait series is the record of Ethan seeing Maya; the exhibition notes are Maya's record of having been seen.
- **Faith as a thread:** From the midnight call (Ch. 3) through the grandmother, Bret's kitchen, the Post-it, the church door in November, the first Sunday with Ethan, Nancy's group, to the wedding. The thread is about the same question as the title: does God see all of you, and is there room?
- **Psalm 139:** The recurring scripture. *"I am fearfully and wonderfully made."* Appears as Maya's handwritten Post-it, as the gallery piece title, and implicitly in Pastor James's sermon.
- **The self-portrait:** Ethan's two-year inability to photograph himself honestly. Introduced Ch. 1; shifts slightly Ch. 7. Not fully resolved on the page.
- **The managing voice:** Present from Ch. 1; silenced for the first time Ch. 5; residually active until after the exhibition.

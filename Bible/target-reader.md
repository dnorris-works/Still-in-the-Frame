# Target Reader — *Still in the Frame*

---

## Primary Reader Profile

**Name (composite persona):** Adrienne
**Age:** 28–42
**Gender:** Primarily women; inclusive of non-binary readers; some male readers who trust literary fiction
**Education:** College-educated or self-educated through voracious reading
**Reading habits:** 20–40 books per year; shops by recommendation, community, and cover/blurb feel; active on BookTok and Goodreads

---

## Who She Is

Adrienne reads because she wants to feel something real. She has grown out of the sanitised romance — the love triangle that resolves without cost, the misunderstanding that stretches a hundred pages — but she has not abandoned the genre. She has upgraded it. She wants the emotional honesty of Colleen Hoover with the intelligence of Emily Henry. She wants to cry, but she wants to *understand* why she is crying.

She is likely a professional or graduate student. She moves through the world with competence and a quiet interior life she does not fully show. She knows, in some wordless way, what it feels like to manage how much of yourself you let people see.

She is not necessarily intersex, not necessarily religious, not necessarily an artist. But she knows what it is to carry something she has never said aloud to the person she most wants to tell. She knows the particular arithmetic of trust — how much you give, how much you hold back, and the specific terror of the moment those calculations stop working.

She has loved someone who was not ready to receive what she was offering. She has also, at some point, been that person — the one who couldn't receive it yet.

---

## What She Reads

- **Colleen Hoover:** *It Ends With Us*, *Ugly Love*, *Confess* — she knows and likely loves at least two of these
- **Emily Henry:** *People We Meet on Vacation*, *Book Lovers* — she appreciates wit and intelligence in her heroines
- **Jojo Moyes:** *Me Before You*, *The Last Letter from Your Lover* — she is not afraid of stories that cost something
- **Taylor Jenkins Reid:** *The Seven Husbands of Evelyn Hugo* — she reads character-driven stories where the reveal is the engine
- **Probably also:** *Normal People* (Sally Rooney), *The Midnight Library* (Matt Haig), *Eleanor Oliphant is Completely Fine* (Gail Honeyman)

She knows literary fiction but reaches for it with intention, not habit. She does not want to be challenged — she wants to be *moved*.

---

## Why She Picks Up This Book

The title is quiet but specific. The cover suggests two people, something withheld, something developing — like a photograph. The blurb gives her: a woman with a secret, a man who doesn't look away, a love story that asks what it costs to be completely known. She has read enough to know that premise delivers either manipulation or something true. She is willing to find out.

She may also come to it through the intersex conversation in broader culture — a reader who has a family member, a friend, or her own awareness of the subject, and who wants fiction that handles it as a person rather than a condition. But she does not need to know anything about intersex identity to pick this up. The blurb doesn't lead with it.

---

## What She Needs From This Story

1. **A heroine she can inhabit.** Maya must be smart, specific, and recognisable as a person before she is recognisable as a symbol. Adrienne needs to feel she could have gone to college with Maya, argued with her, loved her.

2. **A love interest who earns it.** She has read enough romances to be sceptical of the man who accepts everything without pause. Ethan's acceptance must feel earned and real — a choice that costs something, made by a person who has considered the cost.

3. **Emotional precision.** She does not want to be told what to feel. She wants the scene built so carefully that the feeling arrives on its own. The hallway floor. The kitchen floor. The overlook. The Post-it note. She will carry these images after the book ends.

4. **Something she didn't know she believed until she read it.** This is the book that makes an argument through love — that the categories are human, that the person is real, that being seen does not require being explained. She may not be able to articulate this argument after she closes the book. She will feel it. That is enough.

5. **Faith handled honestly.** She may be a believer, a lapsed believer, or someone who grew up outside faith entirely. What she cannot tolerate is faith weaponised or dismissed. She needs faith treated the way Maya treats it — as a wound that wants to be a relationship again. She needs the church in this story to be the church that makes room, not the church that doesn't.

## Secondary Reader Profiles

### The Intersex Community Reader
A reader — or their family member or close friend — with a personal connection to intersex experience. They arrive with a heightened need for accuracy, dignity, and specificity. They are alert to the difference between a character who is written *about* and a character who is written *as*. This book does not owe them a happy ending; it owes them a real one. It pays that debt.

### The Faith-Curious Reader
A reader who has left a church, is questioning one, or grew up outside religion and has always held the door open a crack. They come for the love story and find a faith arc that does not resolve into easy answers — it resolves into belonging. Maya's journey from the Post-it to the wedding pew is theirs too, if they want it.

### The Literary Romance Reader
A reader who follows the genre closely — BookTok, Goodreads, romance blogs — and is actively seeking literary fiction that does not condescend to the romance readership. This book sits at the intersection of contemporary literary fiction and emotionally intelligent romance. It belongs on the shelf between *Normal People* and *It Ends With Us* without apology.

### The Male Reader (Smaller Audience, Worth Knowing)
A man who reads widely, who was handed this book by someone who loved it, or who came to it through the arts/photography angle of the cover and description. He will find Ethan's arc more personal than he expected. He will not tell many people he cried.

---

## What This Book Is Not For

- Readers who want a clean, uncomplicated HEA with no difficult subject matter
- Readers who need explicit intersex education rather than immersive fiction
- Readers who object to frank but purposeful physical intimacy
- Readers who need faith content to be either absent or triumphant

---

## Platform and Discovery

- **KDP / Amazon:** Keywords: contemporary romance, literary romance, intersex fiction, emotionally complex romance, found family, faith and love
- **Barnes & Noble:** Shelf placement — literary fiction adjacent to romance; comp titles *It Ends With Us*, *The Seven Husbands of Evelyn Hugo*
- **BookTok/Instagram:** The emotional landing points (the hallway scene, the church door scene, the overlook proposal) are shareable moments that readers will quote. The title and central metaphor give influencers a hook.
- **Book clubs:** The faith arc, the Eileen storyline, and the question of what it costs to be fully known are rich discussion territory. A book club guide would serve this readership well.

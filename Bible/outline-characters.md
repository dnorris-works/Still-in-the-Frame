# Seen and Still Loved — Bible: Characters, Settings & Themes

## Character Profiles

### Maya Vasquez (protagonist)
- Age 26, art history graduate student
- Thesis: "Hidden Narratives in Abstract Expressionism"
- Works part-time as a gallery guide at City Museum of Contemporary Art
- Hiking enthusiast
- Intersex — presents fully as a woman; also has a penis; cannot get pregnant
- Raised Catholic; her faith was stripped by shame — she believes God cannot accept her as she is
- Her mother knows but has never spoken of it; her father believes it was "corrected" at birth
- Past: ex-boyfriend Bret reacted with disgust and cruelty when she disclosed — "You're a freak"
- Only person who knows her full truth: best friend and roommate Zoe
- Her art history thesis is unconscious self-portrait — she studies artists who hid truth in plain sight
- Arc: from hiding and shame → disclosed, accepted, loved, spiritually reclaimed

### Ethan Calloway
- Age 28, exhibition designer at City Museum of Contemporary Art
- Self-taught photographer; hobby darkroom for black and white film
- Introverted, warm, patient — sees things others miss
- Lost his mother at 19 to an aneurysm; she was also an amateur photographer
- His mother's influence: photograph the space between people, not just the people
- Close to sister Rachel; friend to Jake
- Raised loosely Protestant; not practicing but spiritually open
- Arc: learns patience and unconditional acceptance; questions whether photographing Maya is exploitative; asks permission; his love helps her find her way back to God

### Zoe (roommate, best friend)
- Psychology grad student
- The only person who has known Maya's truth for years
- Protective but never controlling — trusts Maya's process
- Steady, warm, occasionally blunt
- Watches Ethan carefully before approving

### Jake (Ethan's colleague)
- Exhibition preparator at the museum
- Outgoing, perceptive despite the bluster
- Has known Ethan 8 years — notices every change in him
- Gives loud advice; usually right

### Rachel (Ethan's sister)
- Age 24, middle school art teacher
- Warm, immediate, perceptive
- Notices Maya's guardedness before Ethan does
- Tells Maya: "Whatever you're carrying, he can handle it."
- Eventually becomes a genuine friend and sister figure to Maya

### Dr. Dana Bowen
- Maya's thesis advisor
- Sees Maya's intellectual power; proud of her
- Appears at thesis presentation and later at gallery events

### Pastor James
- Progressive pastor, Riverside Community Church
- Affirming theology — God made all of creation; intersex people are not mistakes
- Becomes spiritual mentor to Maya after she finds the church

### Reverend Nancy Griffin
- Co-pastor, Riverside Community Church
- Intersex herself — the living proof that Maya is not alone
- Shares her own journey; invites Maya to support group

### Bret (antagonist, backstory)
- Maya's ex-boyfriend, two years prior
- His reaction to her disclosure: disgust, cruelty, "You're a freak. You deceived me."
- Reappears at Ethan's exhibition — threatens to expose her
- His arc ends with deflation: seeing Maya surrounded by love, his power dissolves

## Settings

- **City Museum of Contemporary Art** — where they meet and both work
- **The Palette** — café where they have their first coffee date
- **Riverside Hiking Trail** — second date, first kiss
- **Ethan's apartment** — converted warehouse, darkroom in the bathroom, prints on a wire
- **Maya and Zoe's apartment** — Victorian house, eclectic, where hard conversations happen
- **The Hidden Gallery** — small independent gallery; theme "Concealed/Revealed"; pivotal
- **Riverside Community Church** — progressive, affirming; Maya's spiritual homecoming
- **University conference room** — Maya's thesis presentation

## Themes

- Art as revelation: how we see vs. how we are seen
- Hidden truth in plain sight — Maya's thesis mirrors her life
- Intersex identity as wholeness, not contradiction
- God's love affirms all of creation — faith reclaimed, not abandoned
- The space between people: Ethan's mother's photography philosophy
- Unconditional acceptance — romantic, communal, spiritual
- Past wounds (Bret, church, family silence) vs. present healing
- Reclaiming narrative: from shame to pride, hiding to visibility
- Photography and art as metaphors for truly seeing someone

## Faith Thread — How It Runs Through the Story

The faith arc must be present from the beginning, not dropped in at the end.

- **Act 1 (Ch 1–12):** Small seeds. Maya passes a church and keeps walking. She owns a Bible she hasn't opened. She hears something that makes her think of God and immediately closes the thought. Ethan mentions his mother believed in something — he's not sure what. The faith thread is background noise, not yet a scene.
- **Act 2 (Ch 13–24):** Faith becomes an active wound. Maya stands outside Riverside Church unable to enter (Ch 14). She researches what the church teaches about intersex people — finds mostly rejection. She finds one affirming theologian online and reads it three times. Her thesis argument (hidden truth as valid form of honesty) begins to feel like a theology as much as an art theory.
- **Act 3 (Ch 25–31):** Faith reclaimed. After the revelation and Ethan's acceptance, Maya is able to face the church question. She and Ethan attend together (Ch 19 — Part 2). Pastor James's sermon. Nancy Griffin. The support group. The wound closes. The wedding is held in the church — both romantic and spiritual completion.

# Ethan Calloway

**Role:** Male lead
**Family:** Sister — Rachel Calloway

---

## Physical

Not described in close physical detail. Moves with quiet competence — unhurried, comfortable in spaces, good with his hands. Wears a good jacket when the occasion calls for it.

---

## Profession

Exhibition designer at the City Museum of Contemporary Art. His work is lighting, spacing, and sequencing — how a work is seen changes everything about how it is received. He believes his job is not to display art but to curate the *relationship between the viewer and the work.* He will pursue the right solution past official channels rather than wait for resolution.

---

## Photography

Shoots black-and-white film exclusively. His primary subject is people — specifically the *space between* people, inherited directly from his mother's practice. He has a home darkroom built by his mother in a converted bathroom. He photographs instinctively and candidly; he will raise the camera before he has decided to, and acknowledge the intrusion after. He asks permission when a photograph matters to him.

---

## Personality

Patient, observant, precise. He says the true thing rather than the expected thing. He doesn't fill silences — he waits in them. He files information rather than discarding it: small details about people, the way light falls, the way a print is developing. He is not demonstrative, but he is completely present.

He is capable of failure. When doubt arrives he will sit inside it instead of moving toward the person who needs him. He knows this about himself.

---

## Interior Life

Ethan has been trying to understand something for two years without quite finding the vocabulary for it. The self-portraits are the evidence: he has been making them for two years and they are all technically correct and essentially false — he looks like someone performing the idea of himself. He is not shut down; he is looking for what he is missing.

He is interested in connections. His mother's belief — that the space between people is where real things happen — is something he inherited before he had language for it, and it shapes how he moves through the world.

---

## Faith

Does not know what he believes. Thinks about it. His mother's personal, non-churchgoing faith is his only reference point. His family attended church as children — Rachel still goes occasionally; he has drifted but has never entirely closed the door.

---

## His Mother

She died when he was nineteen from a sudden aneurysm. He was the one who found her — he was home for the weekend. She was a passionate amateur photographer who built the darkroom and photographed the space between people. She said: *"The right light doesn't just show you what's there — it shows you what was always there but hidden."* She had a simple, personal faith. Her portrait — smiling, silver frame — sits on the shelf above his desk.

---

## His House

Books sorted by subject. A large black-and-white print on the living room wall — a woman with her back to the camera, standing at a window, light falling across her shoulders. Prints clipped to a wire running the length of the hallway (coat hangers and eye hooks). A kitchen that has been cooked in.

---

## Voice

Plain. Not verbose. He says what he means without dressing it up. His questions are real — he asks them because he wants the answer. He doesn't use compliments as social currency; when he says something is good he is reporting an observation.

---

## Key Relationships

- **Maya Vasquez** — The person around whom everything in him that has been looking becomes still.
- **Rachel Calloway** — His sister. An art teacher. Five inches shorter than him, same dark eyes. She knows him the way only family does.
- **Jake** — His closest friend, eight years. Loud, loyal, tends to disguise his own discomfort as care for others.

# Reverend Nancy Griffin

**Role:** Co-officiant at Riverside Community Church / support group convener
**Status:** Active — first appears Chapter 21

---

## Physical

In her sixties. Solidly built. Wears a clerical collar under a cardigan in a shade of green that has clearly been chosen because she likes it rather than because it is appropriate. Her face is that of someone who has listened to a great many difficult things and has not been diminished by any of them. At the wedding, the green cardigan is replaced by something ceremonial, though the authority in her face remains exactly the same.

---

## Personality

She moves quietly in rooms where things matter. She is present before you notice she has arrived. She says things in a normal voice, as plain fact, in contexts where other people would lower their voice — including: *"I've sat with a lot of people the church didn't know what to do with. God always did. It was the institution that got confused."* She has no interest in performing authority because she does not need it. She is not unkind — she is economical with words and trusts people to receive them without packaging.

---

## Faith

She is not sentimental about the church's history with people like those in her group. She acknowledges the institution's failure plainly and without apology. She holds the theology separately from the institution's record on it, and this is not a tension she needs to manage — it is simply how she understands things.

---

## The Group

She runs a Wednesday evening gathering at St. Anne's basement, 7pm, door on the side street. She delivers the invitation as a single index card with no explanation of what the group is, no phone number, and no obligation. She has been doing this long enough to know that too much information gives people too many reasons not to come.

She convenes rather than leads. She holds the room, asks a question, and lets it be answered in pieces. She knows when to open a door and when to let it stay shut.

---

## Connection to Maya's Story

She reads Maya's Voss paper (via Douglas Reid) and recognises something in it. She approaches Maya at Riverside Community Church — not intrusively — after the morning service and introduces herself. She tells Maya about the Wednesday group. She is the person who, without drama, makes room where room has not previously existed.

She co-officiates the wedding. Her blessing: *brief, precise, the theology of it carried in four sentences that said everything and explained nothing.* She had been waiting for this particular outcome longer than the couple knew.

---

## Key Relationships

- **Maya Vasquez** — She sees her walking in and greets her with the ease of someone for whom Maya's presence is unsurprising. She is waiting for this conversation without having been told it was coming.
- **The group** — Jordan, Diane, Clare, and others (including the younger Priya). She holds them with authority that comes from long practice and genuine care.

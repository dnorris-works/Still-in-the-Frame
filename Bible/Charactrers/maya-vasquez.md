# Maya Vasquez

**Role:** Female lead
**Identity:** A trans woman

---

## Physical

Dark jeans, consistently. Her hair loose or pulled back, a few strands at the side of her face. Her head tilts slightly when she is thinking. She holds a pen she is not using. She moves through galleries with her hands behind her back, the way she moves through anything she is trying to understand properly.

---

## Profession

Part-time gallery guide at the City Museum of Contemporary Art, and PhD candidate. Her thesis — *Hidden Narratives in Abstract Expressionism* — argues that certain artists embedded personal histories into their *technique* rather than their subject matter. The technique itself is the narrative. Concealment is not a failure of honesty but a different, slower kind — one that trusts the right person to find it eventually. Her central case study is Elara Voss, a nearly unknown German painter of the 1920s.

She is at home at a lectern. The version of her that stands in front of a room is the least managed version — completely without fear.

---

## Personality

Private, precise, self-aware. She processes internally and returns to things alone. She challenges people when she suspects they are not being genuine. When she decides something she does not hedge. She is funnier than people expect — her wit is dry and exact, and she deploys it without warning. She does not fill silences.

She has been managing herself carefully since she was seventeen — building a version of herself that works, that doesn't require explaining, that can find the exit before anyone finds her out. She is very good at this. It has cost her everything it was protecting.

---

## Interior Life

Maya's intelligence is not cold — it is vigilant. She has spent years mapping her own patterns, naming what she carries, reading silences with precision. She knows when she is being managed and she knows when she is managing herself.

The thing that shifts in her — the thing the whole story turns on — is the moment she tells the managing voice to be quiet and does not take the exit. She has never done this before.

---

## Faith

She used to believe. It stopped, somewhere around sixteen or seventeen, in a way she describes as complicated. Her grandmother modelled a quiet, undefended faith — the kind that never needed defending because it had never been threatened. She described God not as a judge but as someone who already knew you completely and had decided you were worth knowing. Her phrase to Maya, said when Maya was twelve and scraped her knee on the church steps: *"He sees all of you, love. Not the parts you show. All of it."*

Maya has spent years trying to understand how a person gets to that. Whether it is something you are born into or something you arrive at. Whether it is still available to someone who has already lost it.

---

## Her Grandmother

A retired schoolteacher from County Kilkenny. Moved to the city when Maya's mother was small and never quite adjusted to it. Kept a south-facing balcony garden and grew things that had no business growing there. Died quietly in her sleep, two years before the story's present. Simple, unchallenged faith.

---

## Bret

A figure from her past. His response to her — looking at her with disgust — became the template against which she measures every moment of vulnerability. The managing voice was built in direct response to him. She has been running it since she was seventeen.

---

## Voice

Precise. She says the true thing plainly, which can sound blunt until you realise she is just being accurate. She challenges rather than deflects. She is funnier than she lets on. She does not perform warmth or enthusiasm — when she gives them they are real. A full stop at the end of a text message is its own kind of communication.

---

## Key Relationships

- **Ethan Calloway** — The first person she has trusted with everything. She tells him she loves him before she has consciously decided to.
- **Zoe** — Her roommate and the person she tells everything to. Maya always tells Zoe.
- **Her grandmother** — Present through memory. The model for the faith she is slowly reconsidering.
- **Bret** — Absent. Present in everything.

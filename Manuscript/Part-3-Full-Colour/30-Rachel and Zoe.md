# Chapter 30: Rachel and Zoe

Rachel called the morning after the café.

Not a text — a call, which was Rachel's way of saying that a text was insufficient for the occasion. Maya answered it on the way to campus and stood on the pavement outside the humanities building with her bag on her shoulder and the April sun doing its best and heard Rachel say, before Maya had finished her hello: *"He texted me last night. When were you going to tell me?"*

"I was going to tell you today."

"Today. Today is very nearly too late."

"It's eight forty-five in the morning."

"I have been awake since six. I have been extremely patient." A pause. "Maya. Let me see the ring."

"I'm on a street."

"I don't care. Hold your hand up."

Maya held her hand up against the building opposite. The dark green stone caught the light.

She heard Rachel make a sound that was somewhere between a gasp and a laugh.

"The *stone*," Rachel said.

"I know."

"He found Mum's brooch."

"He didn't — it's not the same piece. He said he didn't plan it. He just—"

"He just chose the same stone." Rachel's voice had gone quieter. "Without knowing."

"Yes."

A silence. Then: "She would have loved you. I want you to know that. Not just because you make him happy. Because of who you are. She would have seen you — the real you — and she would have loved you."

Maya stood on the pavement outside the humanities building with her bag on her shoulder and did not trust her voice for a moment.

"I know," she said, when she could.

"Good." Rachel cleared her throat. "Now. Wedding. What do we know."

"Riverside Community Church. Probably autumn. Nothing else is decided yet."

"I'm his best person."

"Obviously."

"I have opinions about flowers."

"Rachel."

"Not yet. I'll save them. Maya?" A beat. "I'm so happy for you. Both of you. I'm so—" Her voice wobbled slightly and then recovered, with the effort audible. "You're going to be my sister and I have wanted a sister my entire life and I want you to know that I knew. When I met you that first weekend. I knew."

"You didn't say anything."

"I was waiting," Rachel said. "Some things you wait for. This was one of them." A pause. "And I know everything now. Ethan told me, and then I was at the exhibition and I saw — all of it. And I want you to hear me say: it is all the same to me. You are all the same to me. Just so that's said and we don't have to carry it."

Maya's throat tightened again.

"Thank you," she said. For the second time. It was still the only possible thing.

"Right," Rachel said, her voice back to its usual forward momentum. "Flowers."

---

Maya told Zoe on a Friday night.

She had been going to tell her earlier — had drafted the text, deleted it, drafted it again, determined finally that this was not a text and went home instead. Zoe was in the kitchen when she arrived, Marcus apparently elsewhere, making something that involved a cutting board and a glass of wine she had already started. She looked up when Maya came in and then looked again in the way she always did when something was different.

"Sit down," Maya said. "I have something to show you."

Zoe set down the knife.

Maya held up her left hand.

Zoe looked at the ring for three full seconds without speaking, which was not something Zoe often did — three seconds of silence was, for her, a significant interval. Then she made a sound that was neither a word nor quite a cry and came around the counter and took Maya's hand and held it and looked at the ring at close range and then looked up at Maya's face and then she sat down on the kitchen floor.

Not dramatically. Just — she sat down, because the kitchen floor was apparently where her legs had decided they were going.

Maya sat down across from her.

Zoe was crying. She had not announced this. It had simply happened, the way things happened with Zoe — fully, without management, the emotion present in her face and her eyes and the set of her mouth, all of it real and unperformed.

"Zoe."

"I'm fine." She was clearly fine in the way of someone in the middle of something. She pressed the back of her hand to her eyes. "I'm completely fine. Don't look at me."

"You sat down on the kitchen floor."

"The floor seemed right." She blinked. Looked at the ring again. "Tell me everything. Start from the beginning. No — start from the trail. I want the trail."

Maya told her the trail. The flask of coffee he'd brought because she'd mentioned the café once, nearly a year ago. The hawk. His name, the way he said it, the way she understood before she saw the ring. His face on one knee on the flat rock with his eyes already wet. *Yes. Obviously yes.*

Zoe listened without interrupting, which was also not a thing she usually did. She sat on the kitchen floor with her wine forgotten on the counter above her and listened to the whole of it, and when Maya finished she was quiet for a moment.

"He brought coffee," she said. "Because you mentioned the café once."

"Nearly a year ago."

Zoe looked at the ceiling. "I've been watching him see you for months," she said. "Since the beginning. The way he looks at you. I kept waiting for you to let him."

Maya was quiet.

"I kept waiting for him to stop," she said.

Zoe looked at her directly. "He's not going to stop."

The words landed the way true things landed — simply, without effort, because there was nothing to argue with in them.

"No," Maya said. "I don't think he is."

Zoe reached forward and took her hand — the left one, the one with the ring — and held it in both of hers.

"I've been watching you hide for three years," she said. "I watched you get good at it. I watched you make peace with the idea that this—" She tilted her head, meaning the ring, meaning Ethan, meaning all of it. "—wasn't going to be available to you. That you'd been through Bret and the cost was too high and you were going to be okay without it." A pause. "I never believed you. But I didn't push because it wasn't mine to push."

"Zoe."

"I'm not finished." She squeezed Maya's hand. "You are the bravest person I have ever known. Not because of the exhibition, though that too. Because you let him in. Knowing what it cost last time. Knowing what it meant. You let him in anyway." She looked at the ring one more time. "And he was worth it."

Maya's throat was tight.

"He was worth it," she agreed.

Zoe finally reached up and retrieved her wine from the counter without standing, which required a significant reach and a certain lack of dignity that broke the gravity of the moment cleanly. She held it up.

"To Ethan," she said. "Who is, for the record, extremely good-looking and also apparently perfect, and I say this as someone who has always maintained that perfect men are a myth."

"He's not perfect."

"He brought you coffee because you mentioned a bad café eleven months ago. He's perfect."

Maya laughed. "He had a day of silence in Part One."

"I'm choosing to forget that." Zoe raised the glass slightly higher. "To Ethan. And to you. And to the wedding at the church, which I assume is happening because you've been going every Sunday for six months and you have that look."

"What look."

"The look of someone who has found something they thought they'd lost and is being very careful not to break it by looking directly at it." Zoe took a sip. "It's a good look. It suits you."

Maya reached up and retrieved the second glass from the counter, with the same undignified reach, and held it up.

"To the church," she said.

"To the church," Zoe said. "And to Pastor James, who is lovely, and to Nancy Griffin, who is terrifying in the best way, and to the fact that you, Maya Vasquez, are getting married in six months and I have a dress to find."

They sat on the kitchen floor with their wine and the cutting board abandoned on the counter and the Friday evening doing its gentle thing outside the window, and talked for two hours about nothing that mattered and everything that did — the dress, the flowers, Rachel's opinions about flowers which Zoe declared she was fully prepared to support, Marcus's dancing which she was not prepared to defend, the photographs Ethan would take and how they would look in fifty years, the vows which Maya said she hadn't written yet and Zoe said she didn't believe.

At some point the wine was gone and the evening was late and Zoe leaned her head back against the cabinet and looked at the ceiling.

"I knew he was the one," she said. "That first morning. When you came out of your room after the museum and you had that face."

"What face."

"The face." She turned her head. "The one you had when you were about to stop hiding. I'd never seen it before. I recognised it anyway." She smiled. "You looked terrified and completely certain at the same time."

Maya thought about that.

"I was," she said.

"I know." Zoe closed her eyes. "That's how you know it's real."

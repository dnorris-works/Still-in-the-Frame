# Chapter 22: The Photo Shoot

Maya arrived at the museum's photography studio at ten, Ethan beside her, the small bag in her hand lighter than she'd expected for what it contained — a robe, some water, the printed reference images Michelle had sent over for the shoot. She had packed and repacked it the night before.

The studio was already set when they arrived. Backdrops — solid black, soft grey — positioned on the far wall. Equipment table along one side. The lighting was soft and even, the kind that made a room look more considered than it was. Michelle Adams shook their hands and offered coffee, which Maya declined.

She walked the space slowly while they talked logistics. She did this with galleries too — checked the sightlines, got the feel of how it moved. This was different from a gallery. A gallery held other people's truth. This room was going to hold hers.

"Maya." Michelle was watching her. "You're in complete control here. We stop when you say stop, we adjust anything that doesn't feel right."

"I know," Maya said. "Thank you."

She did know. She had thought about little else for three weeks.

---

For the first image, she removed her robe and stood where Michelle directed her — hands and knees on the studio floor, one arm extended upward, reaching toward something above the frame. She had been told the composition in advance. She had practised the position in her bedroom the night before, alone, feeling for what it would cost.

It cost less than she expected and more than she'd admitted.

The first few minutes were simply discomfort — the physical awkwardness of a pose held for attention, the self-consciousness of being observed, the awareness of the camera's aperture as something that included everything the eye could not be asked to overlook. She had thought she was past this. She had stood naked in front of Ethan and looked him in the face and felt no shame. This was different. This was permanent.

"How does this feel?" Michelle asked between frames.

"Like prayer," Maya said without moving. "Like the kind of prayer I used to be afraid to say."

She meant it, which surprised her. The reaching wasn't performance. It was exactly what it looked like.

She held the position and let the camera do what the camera did. At some point the self-consciousness dissolved — not dramatically, not in a moment she could mark later, but gradually, the way her body had stopped being a source of shock when she was finally with someone who didn't treat it as one. She was just reaching. She was just here.

"That's beautiful work," Michelle said eventually. "Take a break."

---

The second photo required Ethan.

He joined her without ceremony, kneeling beside the backdrop with his arm extended toward hers, two inches between their fingers. The rehearsal they'd done at home for positioning had made it easier to find — the exact place where the gap between them was visible but small, where the reach was still reaching and hadn't yet become arrival.

Maya held the pose and looked at his hand. She thought: *this is the moment before I wasn't alone anymore.* She hadn't known it was that moment when it was happening. She had been afraid, in Ethan's apartment, on his couch, that the telling would end something. That the relief would be followed by the thing that always followed — the reordering of someone's face, the careful recalibration.

His face had not reordered. His hand, four inches from hers across a studio floor, was entirely steady.

Michelle was shooting. Maya held still.

"You can see the story," she said when they reviewed the shots afterward. "From reaching for the divine to reaching for love."

"And they're not separate things," Ethan said quietly.

She looked at him. He was looking at the images, not at her. She thought: *I know.*

---

The third photo required something she hadn't anticipated — not courage, exactly. She had prepared for courage. She had given herself speeches about visibility and representation and the people who would see these photographs and understand themselves differently because of them. The speeches were true and they were beside the point.

What the third photo required was willingness to be finished hiding. Not the decision — she had made the decision weeks ago. The living of it. The actual standing in front of the camera and facing the aperture with everything visible and letting that be so.

She removed her robe and handed it to Ethan. He folded it carefully and set it aside. She turned and faced Michelle's camera, arms at her sides, chin lifted slightly — the posture she had practised and which felt, in this moment, both studied and entirely her own.

"Ethan," she said.

He moved behind her, his arms coming around her waist, his chest against her back. His face appeared over her shoulder. She felt him settle, still, his hold firm without being held.

The camera clicked.

She didn't think about anything for a while. She stood and breathed and let the light do what light did. Somewhere in the middle of it something shifted — the line between decision and fact, between having chosen this and simply being someone who had. She didn't know when it crossed. She knew it had.

"I think we have it," Michelle said finally.

Maya turned in Ethan's arms.

They looked at each other. She could feel the heaviness in her chest and also the absence of the thing that had lived there for years — the managing, the angling, the careful pre-emptive editing of herself. There was just the room and his face and the light that had been documenting all of it.

"Can we see?" she said.

Michelle brought the images up on the display. Maya looked at them. Her own face, fully visible. Ethan's arms. The composition she had described in a museum director's office months ago and which had now, simply, happened.

She had spent so many years hiding — from doctors, from lovers, from God. From herself.

These photographs were the opposite of hiding.

She looked at them for a long time without speaking. There was nothing she needed to add.

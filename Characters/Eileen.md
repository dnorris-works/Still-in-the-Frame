# Eileen — Character Profile
## Maya's Mother

---

## Basic Information
- **Full name:** Eileen (surname shared with Maya)
- **Age:** Mid-to-late fifties (at time of present story)
- **Role:** Maya's mother; X-linked carrier of the CAIS gene
- **Status:** Divorced from Maya's father; lives alone

---

## The Medical Fact
- Eileen is an X-linked carrier of Complete Androgen Insensitivity Syndrome (CAIS)
- She has no symptoms and no outward sign of the condition — she is chromosomally XX and presents as a typical woman
- She was informed by doctors when Maya was approximately three weeks old
- The language used would have been clinical: *carrier*, *your gene*, *passed to your child*
- She absorbed this as: **I did this to her**
- She did not choose to carry the gene; she could not have known; guilt does not operate on logic

---

## Why She Stayed Silent
- The guilt was unsurvivable if named — silence was the only way to keep functioning as a mother
- She also received the standard medical advice of the era: do not tell the child, raise her as a girl, this is a private matter
- She was failed by the medical system before Maya was ever failed by it
- Every year the silence compounded — breaking it would have meant admitting she had known all along, which felt like a second betrayal on top of the first
- She showed her love in every other way she could find: presence, practicality, care
- She was not cold. She was someone carrying a thing too heavy to put down and too heavy to carry, and she chose to carry it alone rather than share the weight with Maya

---

## What She Feared Most
- That her daughter would never be loved completely
- That the condition she passed on had closed that door for Maya
- This fear sat underneath the guilt and made the silence worse — because if she named the condition, she also had to name the fear, and she could not bear to

---

## What Breaks Her Open
- Seeing Ethan with Maya — not at a dramatic moment, but in an ordinary one
- The way he looks at her; the way he touches her arm without thinking; the particular quality of his attention
- She realises: the thing she feared most did not happen
- Relief and grief arrive at exactly the same time
- The relief: Maya is loved. Completely. By someone who knows.
- The grief: twenty-six years of silence that was never necessary

---

## The Apology Scene
- **Placement:** Act 3 — after the proposal is announced, before the wedding
- **Setting:** A private moment, not witnessed by Ethan or Zoe
- She begins speaking and cannot finish — she is not a woman who gives speeches
- Something like: *"I knew. Before you did. They told me when you were three weeks old and I —"* and then silence
- She does not need to complete the sentence. Maya understands.
- Maya's response is not required to be forgiveness. Taking her hand may be enough, for now.
- This is not a resolution. It is an opening.

---

## The Wedding
- Eileen is in the front row
- She does not look away — not once
- She cries before Ethan does
- No words pass between her and Maya about what was said in the earlier scene
- They don't need to. The fact of her presence, staying, not looking away — that is what she can give now
- The reader carries the weight of what this cost her

---

## Character Notes
- Warm, not cold — this is essential
- Not weak; it takes a particular kind of strength to carry guilt that size for twenty-six years
- She is not a villain and must never read as one
- The reader should want to shake her and also understand her completely
- Her arc does not end in a grand confession or a full reconciliation — it ends in presence
- She is not redeemed by a speech. She is redeemed by showing up.

---

## Relationship to the Novel's Themes
- The novel is about being seen and still loved
- Eileen saw Maya completely — from the first week of her life — and loved her completely
- Her tragedy is that the seeing and the loving never connected into speech
- Maya's story is about finding someone who could do what her mother couldn't
- Eileen's presence at the wedding is the closing of that loop — not perfectly, not cleanly, but enough

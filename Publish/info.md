250 word description:

Maya Vasquez has spent years perfecting the art of managed distance. As a PhD candidate and gallery curator, she reads hidden narratives in other people's art with precision and care — while keeping her own hidden thing exactly where she put it, at seventeen, in the dark.

Ethan Calloway photographs people in black-and-white film. His working principle is simple: the right light doesn't show you what is there. It shows you what was always there but hidden. He is not the kind of man who announces himself. He is the kind who pays attention.

They meet in a museum after closing time, over a painting neither of them is supposed to be near. What begins there moves fast and carefully at once — the way you move when you are close to something real and afraid of breaking it.

Maya has never disclosed what she carries. Not since the one time she did, and the person she trusted made her feel it should have stayed hidden. She walked away from that night with two losses: the man, and the faith she had been quietly holding onto since her grandmother's kitchen. She has not gone back for either.

Ethan does not flinch. He does not process it in stages or return with a considered decision. He simply stays — and staying, it turns out, is the hardest and rarest thing.

*Seen and Still Loved* is a love story about what it costs to be fully known, and what becomes possible when you finally let someone pay that cost with you.
